How to Write a Resume in 2026: A Step-by-Step Guide

Updated January 2026 · 9 min read · By the ResumeForge Team

Your resume is often the first impression a hiring manager has of you — and you usually have less than ten seconds to make it count. A great resume is not about listing everything you have ever done. It is about telling a clear, focused story that proves you can solve the employer's problem. In this guide, we will walk through how to write a resume from scratch, section by section, with practical examples you can adapt today.

1. Choose the right resume format

Before you type a single word, decide on a structure. There are three common formats, and the right one depends on your career situation:

  • Reverse-chronological — lists your most recent job first. This is the most widely used and recruiter-preferred format. Choose it if you have a steady work history.
  • Functional — groups your experience by skill rather than date. Useful if you are changing careers or have gaps, but many recruiters are skeptical of it.
  • Combination — blends a skills summary with a chronological work history. A solid choice for senior professionals with diverse experience.

For most people, the reverse-chronological format is the safest and strongest option.

2. Add your contact information

Keep this section clean and professional. Include your full name, a professional email address, phone number, city and country, and a link to your LinkedIn profile or portfolio. Avoid putting your full home address, your date of birth, or a photo unless it is customary in your country or industry.

Tip: Use an email address based on your name, such as firstname.lastname@email.com. Quirky addresses from your school days can undermine an otherwise polished resume.

3. Write a compelling professional summary

The professional summary sits at the top of your resume and is your elevator pitch in two to four sentences. It should highlight who you are, your most relevant experience, and the value you bring. Tailor it to the specific role you are applying for.

A simple formula that works:

[Job title] with [X years] of experience in [field]. Skilled in [key skills]. Proven track record of [measurable achievement].

For example: "Customer success manager with 5 years of experience in SaaS. Skilled in onboarding, retention, and account growth. Helped reduce churn by 18% across a portfolio of 120 enterprise clients."

4. Detail your work experience

This is the core of your resume. For each role, list your job title, the company name, location, and dates. Then add three to six bullet points describing what you achieved — not just what you were responsible for.

The difference between a forgettable and a powerful bullet point comes down to results. Compare:

  • Weak: Responsible for managing social media accounts.
  • Strong: Grew Instagram following from 4k to 22k in 12 months, increasing referral traffic by 35%.

Start each bullet with a strong action verb (led, built, launched, reduced, increased) and quantify the impact wherever possible. Numbers make your achievements concrete and believable.

5. List your education

Include your degree, the institution, and the graduation year. If you are a recent graduate, you can add relevant coursework, your GPA (if strong), honors, and extracurricular leadership. As you gain experience, this section should shrink and move below your work history.

6. Highlight your skills

Create a dedicated skills section that balances hard skills (technical, measurable abilities like Python, accounting, or data analysis) with soft skills (communication, leadership, problem-solving). Mirror the language used in the job description — if the posting asks for "project management," use that exact phrase rather than a synonym.

7. Add optional sections that strengthen your case

Depending on your background, consider adding:

  • Projects — great for developers, designers, students, and career changers.
  • Certifications — show ongoing learning and specialized expertise.
  • Languages — valuable for international or customer-facing roles.
  • Volunteer work — demonstrates initiative and values.

8. Format for clarity and ATS compatibility

Recruiters and software both need to read your resume easily. Use a clean, single-column layout with standard headings, consistent fonts, and plenty of white space. Keep it to one page if you have under ten years of experience, and two pages at most for senior roles. Save and send your final document as a PDF to preserve formatting.

9. Proofread, then proofread again

A single typo can signal carelessness. Read your resume out loud, run a spell checker, and ask a friend to review it. Pay special attention to consistency: verb tenses, date formats, and punctuation should match throughout.
