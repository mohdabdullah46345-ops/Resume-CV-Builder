12 Common Resume Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

Updated January 2026 · 8 min read · By the ResumeForge Team

Most resumes are rejected not because the candidate lacks talent, but because of avoidable errors that distract from their strengths. Here are twelve of the most common resume mistakes we see — along with a clear fix for each.

Advertisement

1. Writing duties instead of achievements

Listing responsibilities tells employers what your job was; listing achievements tells them how well you did it. Fix: Rewrite each bullet to focus on results and impact, and add numbers wherever you can.

2. Using a generic, one-size-fits-all resume

Sending the same resume to every job rarely works. Fix: Tailor your summary and top bullet points to each role, echoing the language in the job description.

3. Typos and grammatical errors

Even one typo can suggest a lack of attention to detail. Fix: Use a spell checker, read your resume aloud, and have someone else proofread it before you send.

4. Making it too long

Recruiters skim. A five-page resume buries your best material. Fix: Keep it to one page for early-career roles and a maximum of two pages for senior positions.

5. A vague or missing summary

Opening with "Hardworking professional seeking opportunities" says nothing. Fix: Write a specific summary that names your role, experience, and a standout achievement.

6. Listing irrelevant experience

Your high-school job from a decade ago rarely helps. Fix: Prioritize experience relevant to the target role and trim or summarize the rest.

7. Poor formatting and inconsistency

Mismatched fonts, uneven spacing, and inconsistent date formats look unprofessional. Fix: Use a consistent template — or let a builder handle formatting for you.

8. Including an unprofessional email address

An old nickname-based email undermines your credibility. Fix: Create a simple address based on your name.

9. Adding a photo or personal details unnecessarily

In many countries, photos, age, and marital status can introduce bias and are best left off. Fix: Include them only when it is standard practice in your region or industry.

10. Weak, repetitive action verbs

Starting every bullet with "Responsible for" is dull. Fix: Lead with varied, strong verbs such as launched, optimized, negotiated, and streamlined.

11. Leaving unexplained gaps or confusion

Unexplained employment gaps can raise questions. Fix: Briefly account for significant gaps with relevant activities like freelancing, study, or caregiving.

12. Forgetting to save as a PDF

Editable documents can shift formatting on another computer. Fix: Export your final resume as a PDF so it looks identical everywhere.
