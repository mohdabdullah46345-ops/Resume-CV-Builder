How to Make an ATS-Friendly Resume That Gets Read

Updated January 2026 · 7 min read · By the ResumeForge Team

You could be the perfect candidate and still never reach a human if your resume fails the first gatekeeper: the applicant tracking system. Most medium and large companies use ATS software to collect, scan, and rank resumes before a recruiter sees them. Understanding how these systems read your document is one of the highest-leverage things you can do in a job search.

What is an ATS and what does it actually do?

An applicant tracking system is software that helps employers manage applications. When you submit a resume online, the ATS parses your document into structured data — name, contact details, work history, skills — and stores it in a database. Recruiters then search and filter that database, often using keywords tied to the job requirements. If your resume is hard to parse or missing the right terms, it can be buried or filtered out automatically.

Use a clean, single-column layout

Fancy multi-column designs, text boxes, and graphics often confuse older parsers, which read left to right and top to bottom. A clean single-column structure with clear section headings is the safest way to ensure every line is read in the correct order. Save visual flair for industries where a designer's portfolio matters more than the parsed text.

Stick to standard section headings

Parsers look for familiar labels. Use conventional headings such as "Work Experience," "Education," "Skills," and "Certifications." Creative titles like "Where I've Made Magic" may look charming but can prevent the system from categorizing your content correctly.

Mirror keywords from the job description

This is the single most important ATS tactic. Read the job posting carefully and identify the skills, tools, and qualifications it emphasizes. Then naturally weave those exact terms into your resume where they genuinely apply. If a posting repeatedly mentions "stakeholder management" and "Agile," and you have done both, make sure those phrases appear.

Never stuff keywords dishonestly or hide white text in the background. Modern systems and recruiters catch these tricks, and they can disqualify you instantly.

Choose ATS-safe fonts and formatting

  • Use standard fonts such as Arial, Calibri, Georgia, or Helvetica.
  • Avoid placing critical information in headers, footers, or images — some parsers ignore these.
  • Use simple bullet points (•) rather than decorative symbols.
  • Spell out acronyms at least once, for example "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)."

Save and submit the right file type

Unless the application specifies otherwise, a PDF generated from a text-based document is usually the safest choice because it preserves your formatting and remains machine-readable. Avoid submitting scanned images or screenshots of your resume — these contain no readable text at all.

Test your resume before you apply

A quick self-check: copy the text from your finished resume and paste it into a plain text editor. If the result is jumbled, out of order, or missing content, an ATS may struggle with it too. Clean layouts almost always paste cleanly.
