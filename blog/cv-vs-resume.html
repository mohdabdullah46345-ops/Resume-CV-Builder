CV vs Resume: What's the Difference and Which to Use

Updated February 2026 · 6 min read · By the ResumeForge Team

The words "CV" and "resume" are often used interchangeably, but they can mean very different things depending on where you are in the world and what you are applying for. Choosing the right document — and the right length and content — can make your application far more effective.

What is a resume?

A resume is a concise, targeted summary of your skills, experience, and education, usually one to two pages long. Its goal is to quickly show an employer that you are a strong fit for a specific role. A good resume is tailored to each job and focuses only on the most relevant highlights.

What is a CV?

CV stands for curriculum vitae, a Latin phrase meaning "course of life." A CV is a comprehensive document detailing your entire academic and professional history — including publications, research, presentations, awards, and teaching experience. CVs are typically longer and grow over time.

The key differences at a glance

  • Length: A resume is short (1–2 pages); a CV can run several pages.
  • Purpose: A resume markets you for a specific job; a CV documents your full career and scholarship.
  • Customization: Resumes are heavily tailored per application; CVs stay relatively static.
  • Detail: A CV includes academic specifics that a resume usually omits.

It also depends on your country

Terminology varies by region, which is a common source of confusion:

  • United States & Canada: "Resume" is used for most jobs. "CV" is reserved for academic, scientific, or research roles.
  • UK, Ireland, and much of Europe: "CV" is the standard term for what Americans call a resume — typically a one to two page document.
  • Australia & New Zealand: "CV" and "resume" are often used interchangeably for the same short document.
Always read the job posting carefully. If an employer asks for a "CV" but the role is a standard corporate job in the US, they most likely mean a resume.

Which one should you use?

Use a resume when applying for most corporate, business, technical, or creative jobs — especially in North America. Use a full academic CV when applying for university positions, research grants, fellowships, or postgraduate programs. When in doubt, match the terminology and expectations stated in the job advertisement.
